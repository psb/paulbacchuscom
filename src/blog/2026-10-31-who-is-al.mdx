It is pretty obvious that the above passage is actually talking about AI and not someone named Al. However, many websites (cough, X, cough), and text in general, will use a font that does not sufficiently differtiate between 'I' and 'l', e.g., <span class="no-font-features">'I' and 'l'</span>.

The term 'AI' is everywhere at the moment and so the probability of <span class="no-font-features">'AI'</span> meaning AI is much higher than it meaning Al. Yet, there is always the chance that it could mean Al and you would only know that from the context in which it is used and not from the font if the font doesn't differtiate between 'I' and 'l'. Five years ago the probability that <span class="no-font-features">'AI'</span> meant Al would have been higher. <InlineNote>The foolproof way of eliminating confusion between AI and Al would be to use full stops in the abbreviation of A.I., but most well known abbreviations do not have full stops in them.</InlineNote>

When I was redesigning my website <InlineNote>Yes, this is it. Yes, it is cyan. No, there is no dark mode.</InlineNote> and looking for a font for the main text, the term 'AI' affected my choice because of the potential confusion of things like <span class="no-font-features">AI vs Al</span> (AI vs Al). Had I redesigned my blog before the rise of AI, I probably wouldn't have paid that much attention to the legibility of the letter l.

Programmers have spent many an hour down the font rabbit hole when it comes to chosing a font for their code. Many comparison websites for programming fonts will have a sequence of characters that look similar in regular fonts as a test for the coding font, so that people can see the legibility of the coding font. Compare the letters **<span class="no-font-features">O0 Il</span>**, which letter is a capital O and which is a 0? The same letters in a coding font **`O0 Il`** clearly distinguishses between similar font. The font chosen for this site has font features that can be activated to enhance legibility, so the same sequence as before now looks like **O0 Il**. There are also some fonts designed to maximise legibility, like [Atkinson Hyperlegible](www.brailleinstitute.org/freefont/) from the Braille Institute. <InlineNote>I would have gone with this font but the reverse slashed zero was a legibility step too far for me.</InlineNote>

In the grand scheme of things this legibility issue may seem trivial; but as the talk about AI increases, hopefully someone cares enought to make the text you read more legible. So when <span class="no-font-features">AI</span> kills everyone, don't blame <span class="no-font-features">Al</span>.
